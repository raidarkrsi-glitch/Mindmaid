A little piece of home

Missing home can show up in unexpected ways. It makes sense to miss what feels familiar.

01 · NOTICE

Name what you miss

Is it a person, a smell, a favourite meal, a familiar route, a sound, or a daily routine? You can miss more than one thing.

02 · REFLECT

Take a short visit home

Close your eyes if comfortable. Picture a place that feels like home. Notice one detail — the light, a sound, or a familiar smell. Stay with that memory for a few breaths.

03 · BRING HOME CLOSER

Make a small piece of home

Call someone, make a familiar meal, play a favourite playlist, or keep a comforting object nearby.

04 · BE PATIENT

Let belonging take its time

Homesickness is common, and it often eases as new routines and connections grow. You can miss home and still begin to like where you are.

A gentle prompt

“What's one thing here I'm starting to like?”

☁️

You deserve connection

Loneliness is a human feeling, not a flaw. It doesn't mean there is anything wrong with you.

01 · REMEMBER

You're not the only one

Many people feel lonely sometimes, even when others are around. You don't need to judge yourself for it.

02 · ONE SMALL STEP

Send a simple message

You could text someone, “Hey, I was thinking of you. How are you?” It doesn't have to become a big conversation.

03 · LOW-PRESSURE COMPANY

Be around people gently

Try a library, club, class, shared meal, park, or a familiar public place. You don't have to talk the whole time.

04 · SELF-COMPASSION

Speak to yourself kindly

Place a hand somewhere comfortable and try: “This is a hard moment. I deserve care and connection, just like anyone else.”

A gentle prompt

“Who made me feel seen recently?”

🍃

Let's make things feel a little smaller

You don't have to solve everything at once. Let's slow the moment down.

01 · RESET

One-minute breathing pause

Let your shoulders soften. Breathe in gently for 4 counts and out for 6. Repeat at a comfortable pace; return to normal breathing if it feels uncomfortable.

02 · UNLOAD

Brain dump

Write down everything on your mind. It can be messy, unfinished and out of order. You don't need to fix any of it yet.

03 · SORT

What can I influence?

Separate the list into “can control” and “can't control”. Put your energy toward what is within your reach, and let the rest wait for now.

04 · BEGIN SMALL

Choose one next step

Pick one manageable action. Make it small enough to begin in five minutes or less.

A kind reminder

Rest if you can. Have some water. Step away for a moment. You are allowed to pause.

🫧

You are having a thought — not a certainty

Thoughts can feel loud. We can notice them without having to follow every one.

01 · LABEL

Create a little distance

Try saying: “I'm having the thought that…” This can help you notice a thought as a thought, rather than a fact.

02 · UNLOOP

Write the loop down

Put the repeating thought on paper or in the space below. You don't have to solve it while writing.

03 · GROUND

Return to this moment

Name 5 things you see, 4 things you can feel, 3 things you hear, 2 things you smell, and 1 thing you taste. Skip any sense that isn't comfortable or available.

04 · CHOOSE

Solve or release?

Ask: “Is this a problem I can take a step toward solving, or a worry I can practise releasing for now?”

Try a gentle boundary

Set aside up to 10 minutes to write about the worry. When the time is up, choose one grounding or everyday activity to return to.

🩷

Give yourself room to feel

Loss and heartbreak can bring real grief. You don't have to rush yourself through it.

01 · VALIDATE

Your feelings matter

It is okay if this hurts, takes time, or comes in waves. There is no timetable you have to follow.

02 · EXPRESS

Write an unsent letter

Write what you wish you could say. This letter is for your own reflection; you do not have to send it.

03 · TODAY ONLY

Choose gentle self-care

Pick one caring action for today: eat something, rest, step outside, listen to a comforting song, or do one familiar routine.

04 · DON'T CARRY IT ALONE

Lean on someone you trust

Consider telling a trusted friend, family member, counsellor or another supportive adult how things have been feeling.

A gentle reminder

If the pain feels too heavy to manage alone, you deserve support. Reach out to someone you trust or a qualified professional. If you feel unsafe, contact local emergency services or a trusted adult right away.

🌷

It's okay not to know yet

You don't need the perfect word. We can explore gently, one question at a time.

01 · PERMISSION

No label needed

You are allowed to feel unsure. You can simply begin with “something is going on, and I'm still figuring it out.”

02 · FIND A WORD

Try a feelings check-in

Could it be sadness, worry, anger, relief, excitement, tiredness, or a mix? You can choose more than one — or none.

03 · BODY CHECK-IN

Notice without judging

Ask: “Where do I notice this feeling in my body?” You can simply notice warmth, tightness, lightness, energy, or nothing in particular. You don't need to change it.

04 · WRITE FREELY

Two minutes, no rules

Write whatever comes to mind for two minutes. It doesn't need to make sense or be shared with anyone.

Where could you go next?

You can return to these reflections whenever you need. Be gentle with yourself along the way.
